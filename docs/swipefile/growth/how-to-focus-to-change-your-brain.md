# How to focus to change your brain

**Source:** [How to focus to change your brain](https://youtu.be/4AwyVTHEU3s?si=9tH7Ow2O-Uu46nky)

## I. Introduction to Neuroplasticity (0:00-0:03)

- **Definition:** The brain and nervous system's ability to change itself in response to experience.
- **Capability:** The nervous system is designed for change, allowing us to learn, adapt, and reorganize throughout life.

## II. The Plasticity of the Nervous System (0:03-0:08)

- **Developmental vs. Adult Brain:** Children learn passively without intense focus. Adults (post-age 25) require specific conditions and internal states to trigger plasticity.
- **Non-plastic Circuits:** Systems like heartbeat, breathing, and digestion are intentionally hardwired for reliability and do not change.
- **New Neurons:** There is little to no addition of new neurons in the human brain after puberty; changes occur through the strengthening or weakening of existing connections.

## III. The Role of Attention and Awareness (0:08-0:13)

- **Selective Shift:** The brain does not change from every experience. It requires a selective shift in attention to signal that an event is worth encoding.
- **Mechanisms of Change:** The prefrontal cortex signals to the nervous system that specific information is important, which then releases neurochemicals necessary for synaptic adjustment.

## IV. Neurochemical Requirements for Plasticity (0:13-0:15)

- **Epinephrine:** Released from the Locus Coeruleus, this chemical creates alertness and is the first requirement for change.
- **Acetylcholine:** Released from the nucleus basalis and the parabigeminal nucleus (brainstem), this chemical acts as a spotlight, highlighting specific sensory inputs to increase the signal-to-noise ratio.

## V. Protocols to Access Plasticity (0:15-0:20)

- **Alertness:** Acknowledge that the brain requires physiological arousal to learn. Techniques include achieving a good sleep schedule or using goal accountability (e.g., social commitment, fear of failure, or love-based motivation).
- **Focus:** Avoid reliance on stimulants like nicotine. Instead, train focus by eliminating distractions for distinct windows of time.

## VI. Visual Focus and Mental Attention (0:20-0:26)

- **Principle:** Mental focus follows visual focus.
- **Tool:** To increase mental depth, narrow your visual gaze to a specific target or point in space for 60-120 seconds. This activates the neurological circuits responsible for alertness and attention.
- **Auditory Learning:** If learning via sound, closing your eyes can help create a 'cone' of attention, similar to how visual focus works.

## VII. Learning Bouts and Ultradian Cycles (0:26-0:27)

- **Timing:** Focus is optimized in ~90-minute ultradian cycles.
- **Warm-up:** Allow for a 5-10 minute transition at the start of a learning bout to enter a state of deep focus.

## VIII. Sleep and Consolidation (0:27-0:29)

- **Consolidation:** Neuroplasticity does not occur during learning; it happens during deep sleep and recovery.
- **NSDR:** Non-Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR) or brief naps (20 minutes) immediately following a learning session can accelerate the rate of learning and memory consolidation.
